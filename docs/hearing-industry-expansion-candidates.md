# Hearing-Industry Coverage Expansion Candidates

## Purpose

The repository currently covers three public companies that expose different
parts of the hearing-care market:

- Sonova: hearing aids, consumer hearing products, cochlear implants, and
  vertically integrated hearing-care retail.
- GN: hearing aids plus enterprise and consumer audio.
- Amplifon: global hearing-care retail.

The next additions should make cross-company analysis more useful, rather than
only increase the company count. The strongest candidates either complete the
direct peer set or add an important competitive force such as implants, retail
distribution, or alternative hearing-device form factors.

This assessment reflects information available on June 7, 2026.

## Highest-Priority Addition: Demant

Demant is the strongest next addition because it fills the largest gap in the
current competitive story:

- Its Oticon, Bernafon, and Philips hearing-aid brands compete directly with
  Sonova's Phonak and GN's ReSound portfolios.
- Its Audika and KIND clinic networks compete directly with Amplifon and with
  Sonova's hearing-care retail operations.
- Its reporting separates Hearing Aids, Hearing Care, and Diagnostics, enabling
  analysis of manufacturing performance versus retail performance.
- Its calendar-year reporting aligns with Sonova's competitors more closely
  than a June-year-end company would.

Demant reported that its acquisition of KIND expanded its global hearing-care
network to more than 4,500 clinics. This makes Demant especially useful for
examining whether vertical integration creates retail advantages, channel
conflict, or pressure on independent clinics.

## Next Layer: Cochlear And Fielmann

### Cochlear

Cochlear expands the analysis from conventional hearing aids into implantable
hearing solutions. It directly competes with Sonova's Advanced Bionics while
also partnering with GN on integrated bimodal systems that connect cochlear
implants and ReSound hearing aids.

This combination of competition and cooperation would expose product-category
boundaries, technology-sharing incentives, and the treatment path from hearing
aids to implants.

Primary sources:

- [Cochlear investor relations](https://www.cochlear.com/us/en/corporate/investors)
- [Cochlear and GN expand their R&D partnership](https://www.cochlear.com/au/en/corporate/media/media-releases/media-releases/2025/2025-03-27-cochlear-and-gn-expand)

### Fielmann

Fielmann adds another public retailer and shows how optical chains can expand
into audiology. Its 2025 financial statements reported EUR 153.0 million of
hearing-care revenue, equal to 6.3% of consolidated revenue.

Fielmann is less useful as a direct hearing-sector financial peer today, but it
is useful for analyzing retail convergence, clinician capacity, customer
acquisition, and the strategic value of combining vision and hearing care.

## Disruption Watch: EssilorLuxottica

EssilorLuxottica should be treated as a strategic disruption candidate rather
than a direct financial peer. Its Nuance Audio glasses combine an open-ear
hearing solution with eyewear and target adults with perceived mild-to-moderate
hearing loss.

The company reported that Nuance Audio was available across 16,000 stores in
twelve markets by the end of 2025. That distribution scale could pressure the
traditional model in which hearing-aid manufacturers and audiology clinics
control product discovery, fitting, and follow-up care.

Coverage would help answer whether alternative form factors and optical retail
channels can expand the hearing-care market or take customers from traditional
hearing aids. However, the repository should avoid interpreting group-level
financial results as evidence of Nuance Audio's performance until the company
discloses meaningful hearing-specific metrics.

## Managed Hearing-Care And Benefit Administration

Managed hearing-care organizations administer benefits between health plans,
members, hearing-care providers, and device manufacturers. They can influence
patient volume, product formularies, provider reimbursement, and the economics
of hearing-aid distribution. This is especially relevant in the US Medicare
Advantage market.

The major operators do not justify many new standalone public-company additions:

| Operator | Ownership | Coverage recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amplifon Hearing Health Care | Amplifon | Deepen the existing Amplifon analysis. This is the strongest public-company window into managed hearing care. |
| UnitedHealthcare Hearing and EPIC Hearing Healthcare | UnitedHealth Group | Track as competitive context, but do not add UnitedHealth Group solely for hearing coverage because hearing is immaterial and not separately reported. |
| TruHearing | Private WS Audiology Group | Track as a major competitor and as evidence of manufacturer-benefit-manager vertical integration. |
| Hearing Care Solutions | Integrated into TruHearing on January 1, 2026 | Treat as part of TruHearing rather than a separate candidate. |
| NationsHearing / NationsBenefits | Privately backed NationsBenefits | Track as a supplemental-benefits competitor; public-company financial analysis is unavailable. |

Amplifon Hearing Health Care works with health plans and administers hearing
benefits, including provider credentialing, compliance, member communications,
and claims-related administration. Amplifon states that its network includes
more than 8,800 clinic locations and works with more than 80 plans. This business
deserves a dedicated subsection in Amplifon reports because it gives the company
influence over both patient access and provider economics, not only ownership of
retail clinics.

UnitedHealthcare Hearing is the only meaningful public-parent candidate not
already covered. UnitedHealth Group launched the business by combining EPIC
Hearing Healthcare and hi HealthInnovations. It can show how a payer may
internalize hearing-benefit administration, but UnitedHealth Group is too broad
and does not disclose hearing-specific financial results. It should therefore
be monitored as a market force rather than added to the normal report pipeline.

TruHearing is strategically important despite being private. It reports more
than 160 million covered lives, more than 110 health-plan partners, and a network
of more than 8,850 locations. Because it is part of WS Audiology, it also raises
the same vertical-integration questions as Amplifon and Demant: whether control
of patient access and benefit design affects manufacturer choice and channel
competition.

Recommended managed-care analysis questions:

- Which companies control patient referrals and benefit design rather than only
  manufacture or dispense devices?
- Do benefit managers favor affiliated manufacturers, exclusive products, or
  restricted formularies?
- How do managed-care contracts affect provider reimbursement, service levels,
  and independent-clinic participation?
- Are manufacturers gaining or losing negotiating leverage as Medicare
  Advantage hearing benefits expand?

## Private Competitors

WS Audiology and Starkey are important global competitors, but they should not
be implemented as normal company coverage while the project is focused on
public companies.

- WS Audiology owns brands including Signia, Widex, and Rexton and also operates
  retail and digital distribution businesses. It publishes some financial
  reports but is privately owned.
- Starkey is a privately held global hearing-technology company and does not
  provide public-company-level financial reporting.

These companies should appear as competitive context in peer comparisons, with
clear caveats about limited financial transparency.

## Recommended Expansion Sequence

1. Add Demant to complete the direct public manufacturer comparison and deepen
   vertical-integration analysis.
2. Add Cochlear to connect hearing aids, implants, and GN's bimodal technology
   partnership.
3. Add Fielmann to broaden retail-channel analysis.
4. Add EssilorLuxottica as a disruption watch focused on alternative form
   factors and nontraditional distribution.
5. Reassess RION and Knowles after the peer-comparison model can handle regional
   reporting differences and upstream suppliers.
6. Add managed-care comparisons across company reports, led by deeper analysis
   of Amplifon Hearing Health Care and contextual tracking of UnitedHealthcare
   Hearing, TruHearing, and NationsHearing.

With the first four additions, the repository could tell a coherent sector
story across hearing-aid manufacturing, retail clinics, implants, and emerging
consumer-access channels while keeping private competitors and benefit
administrators visible as context.
